Clinical Evidence

What the Research
Actually Shows

Non-surgical spinal decompression has been validated in randomized controlled trials, peer-reviewed journals, and thousands of clinical cases. The evidence is real and documentable.

01
Pain Reduction & Disc Volume Decrease on MRI

A prospective randomized controlled trial at Seoul National University Bundang Hospital found NSDT produced statistically significant reductions in pain intensity and measurable reduction in herniated disc volume on MRI vs. a pseudodecompression control group. (n=60 patients)

Choi, Pyung-Bok. Int'l Journal of Clinical Practice. 2022. doi:10.1155/2022/6343837
02
Good to Excellent Outcomes for Disc Lesions

A case series in the Journal of Contemporary Chiropractic found non-surgical decompression using the DRX9000 demonstrated good to excellent clinical outcomes for lumbar disc lesions, with pre/post MRI confirming measurable improvements in spinal canal dimensions and disc height.

Connolly et al., Journal of Contemporary Chiropractic, 2025. parker.edu
03
Superior Outcomes vs. Standard PT Alone

A single-blinded RCT found NSDT combined with conventional physical therapy produced significantly greater improvements in pain, lumbar range of motion, muscle endurance, and functional disability compared to physical therapy alone in lumbar radiculopathy patients.

BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders. PMC8924735. doi:10.1186/s12891-022-05196-x
04
Validated in Military Medicine

A retrospective chart review in Military Medicine covering March 2023 to July 2024 examined NSSD as a therapeutic modality for low back pain across multiple clinic types, reporting consistent pain improvements across provider types and settings.

Military Medicine, 2025. doi:10.1093/milmed/usaf116
05
Conservative Care Reduces Surgical Escalation

A 2022 study in Spine found that initial choice of conservative spinal care reduces escalation to surgical intervention among Medicare beneficiaries with chronic low back pain — reinforcing decompression care as a viable first-line alternative to surgery.

Whedon JM et al. Spine. 2022;47:E142-8. doi:10.1097/BRS.0000000000004118
06
The Global Burden of Low Back Pain

The Global Burden of Disease Study 2021 identified low back pain as the leading contributor to years lived with disability globally — affecting 619 million people in 2020, projected to 843 million by 2050. The scale of this market is a generational opportunity for disc specialists.

Ferreira ML et al. Lancet Rheumatol. 2023;5:e316-29. doi:10.1016/S2665-9913(23)00098-X
